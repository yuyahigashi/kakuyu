If you are visiting Inbe for its pottery, why not stay overnight in the town or nearby, rather than leave after a day of gallery visits?

Two names I hear while living locally are Tokiwa Ryokan and Ebisuya Araki Ryokan. Both offer a connection to the area's past and the character of a traditional Japanese inn.

They are not, however, both in Inbe. Tokiwa is in the town itself; Ebisuya Araki is near neighboring Nishi-Katakami Station. These are two options to consider, not a claim that they are the only places to stay.

## Tokiwa Ryokan: Stay in the Pottery Town

For a trip built around walking between studios and galleries, Tokiwa Ryokan is worth considering.

At 1485 Inbe, it is approximately five minutes on foot from Inbe Station. Its official website lists six guest rooms and describes an inn retaining the character of traditional Japanese architecture. [Official website](https://tokiwa-ryokan.jimdosite.com/)

Okayama's official tourism website introduces it as a small inn with a hundred-year history. It may appeal particularly to travelers looking for a place within the town, rather than the facilities or spectacle of a large hotel. [Okayama tourism information](https://www.okayama-kanko.jp/reserve/detail_12384.html)

The inn's own account recalls Bizen potters gathering there to discuss how to sell their work. Some, it says, left their pottery instead of money as payment. Such memories connect ceramics not just with display cases, but with the work and daily lives of people in the town.

**Consider it for:** exploring Inbe on foot and reaching your accommodation without a car.

- Address: 1485 Inbe, Bizen, Okayama.
- Telephone: +81 869-64-2032.
- Reservations and room photographs: [official website](https://tokiwa-ryokan.jimdosite.com/).

## Ebisuya Araki Ryokan: Katakami's History and Food Served on Bizen Ware

Ebisuya Araki Ryokan is in Nishi-Katakami. Take the JR Ako Line one stop from Inbe to Nishi-Katakami; the inn is approximately two minutes away on foot. [Official accommodation information](https://ebisuya-araki.jp/stay/)

Founded as a culinary inn in 1856, it has roots in the maritime trade of Katakami. According to its historical account, the business previously operated as the Ebisuya shipping merchant from the early Edo period before becoming an inn in the final years of that era. [Official history](https://ebisuya-araki.jp/history/)

The same account describes repeated visits by the artist and gastronome Kitaōji Rosanjin, who stayed in the Kaede room. The attraction is not simply a list of famous visitors: it is the inn's connection with exchanges involving food and Bizen pottery.

The accommodation page describes renovated late-Edo-period rooms and meals featuring seasonal local ingredients served with Bizen ware. After looking at pots during the day, dinner offers a different way to appreciate them—as vessels in use.

**Consider it for:** an interest in historic buildings and cultural connections, with dining as part of the journey.

- Address: 1280 Nishi-Katakami, Bizen, Okayama.
- Telephone: +81 869-64-2004.
- Reservations and room photographs: [official website](https://ebisuya-araki.jp/).

## Before You Book

Both invite you to treat accommodation as part of the trip. But the appeal of older architecture is not the same thing as the conveniences of a modern hotel.

Confirm whether your plan includes meals, your arrival time, bathroom arrangements, and any stairs or accessibility requirements. International visitors should also ask in advance about available languages and dietary requests.

Rates and availability vary with dates, occupancy, and plans, so this guide does not quote a fixed price. If you are traveling by rail, make sure your train gets you there in time for check-in and any included dinner.

## Beyond a Day Trip

My impression is that many people visit Inbe for the day, but this is not a statement supported here by overnight-visitor statistics. What I would like to suggest is another possibility: stay nearby and give yourself the following day to explore as well.

Tokiwa places you in Inbe; Ebisuya Araki brings neighboring Katakami's history into the journey. The right choice depends on what you hope to enjoy.

Give yourself not only time to see pottery, but time to spend in the place that surrounds it.
