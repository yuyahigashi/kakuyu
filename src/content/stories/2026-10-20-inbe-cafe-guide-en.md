I am now in my second year of living in Inbe. If someone visiting this pottery town asks me where to take a break, these are the six places I would mention.

I am a nomadic worker and spend a lot of my working time in cafés. But the place a resident wants to use every day is not always the place a traveler should visit once.

This guide combines practical stops for a trip with a resident's honest preferences. The numbers are not rankings. Comments on taste, price, and comfort reflect my own visits and priorities.

## Choose by the Kind of Day You Want

- **Close to the station:** UDO or the museum café.
- **Coffee alongside pottery:** Riho or cafe MUGENAN +.
- **Architecture and welcoming service:** coharuni.
- **An everyday option, particularly by car:** Gusto Bizen.

If you arrive by train, check your return departure before exploring. Gaps between trains can be long at some times of day. Use [JR West's station and timetable information](https://www.jr-odekake.net/smt/eki/top?id=0651106) rather than assuming another train will arrive shortly.

Each stop has an embedded Google map. The accompanying “Photos and directions” link opens its Maps listing; photo availability depends on Google Maps.

## 1. Cafe UDO

**The first place to remember if you arrive by train.**

UDO's greatest advantage is its location inside Inbe Station. It is convenient for a pause after arriving or while waiting to leave. To me, the interior feels polished and a little special.

Despite living here, I have visited only once. For everyday use, it felt expensive for my budget—even compared with cafés I have used in Tokyo.

Choosing somewhere to go every day is different from choosing a stop on a trip. If staying close to your train matters, UDO deserves a place on your shortlist.

Address: 1657-7 Inbe, inside Inbe Station on the first floor. The location is also confirmed by the [Bizen Ware Pottery Association](https://touyuukai.jp/restaurant.html).

## 2. Gallery and Café Riho

**Combine looking at Bizen pottery with a coffee break.**

Riho is somewhere I particularly want visitors to try. It sits on the street of pottery shops and studios north of Inbe Station, making it a natural stop during a walk around town.

Its gallery displays work by Living National Treasure Yamamoto Tōshū and his son Yamamoto Yūichi, among others. “Living National Treasure” refers to a practitioner recognized for exceptional traditional skills, rather than a building or object designated a National Treasure. [Official gallery introduction](https://rihou.co.jp/gallery)

I return regularly. I enjoy both the coffee and the food, and appreciate being able to look at pottery while taking a break. Choose Riho if you want your café stop to feel connected to Inbe.

Address: 1530 Inbe. [Official website](https://rihou.co.jp/)

## 3. Bizen City Museum of Art Café

**Art and a convenient break, right by the station.**

The museum café is another useful stop for visitors. The museum, close to Inbe Station, celebrated its grand opening in July 2025. Exhibition Room 0 and the history exhibition room on the first floor are free to visit; this does not mean that all exhibitions, food, and drinks are free. [Official Okayama tourism information](https://www.okayama-kanko.jp/spot/detail_11099.html)

I have used the café often. Other places sometimes appeal more to my personal taste in coffee, but I appreciate how easy it is to drop in and keep the visit within my budget.

As a guide, the official menu checked for this article lists hot American-style coffee at JPY 450 and hot blended coffee at JPY 500. Check the current menu when visiting. [Official café information](https://bizen-moa.jp/shop_cafe/)

Address: 1659-6 Inbe. Café closures follow the museum's closing days.

## 4. coharuni

**For a day when the space matters as much as the menu.**

The setting is what first stays with me at coharuni. Its official website describes a café designed around a courtyard. I find a similar sense of care in the food and drinks. [Official website](https://coharuni.net/)

It strikes me as a lovely place to spend unhurried time together. I have been back many times, and the warmth of the service is what I remember especially well.

It takes a little more walking than the cafés immediately beside the station. Consider combining it with visits to nearby pottery shops and studios.

Address: 902-3 Inbe. At the time of checking, regular opening hours were Friday–Sunday, 11 a.m.–5 p.m. Check the latest announcements for exceptions.

## 5. Gusto Bizen

**For everyday practicality rather than a uniquely local experience.**

Gusto is a nationwide family-restaurant chain. If you are visiting for something distinctive to Inbe, I would start with the other places here. For drivers, or anyone who finds a familiar chain reassuring, it remains an option.

Recently, I have used it often while working. In my experience, the self-service drink bar makes it an affordable place to spend some time. I do not order food because of my personal dietary choices, so I am not offering a judgment on the cooking.

This is not a promise that prolonged laptop use is permitted. Follow the restaurant's guidance, particularly when it is busy, and check the current price and conditions for ordering the drink bar on its own.

Address: 2392 Inbe. [Official restaurant listing](https://store-info.skylark.co.jp/gusto/map/011711/)

## 6. cafe MUGENAN +

**My personal first choice for the coffee itself.**

The green, hillside surroundings make this somewhere I like to consider for a visit by car. On my visits, I have seen cars from farther afield in the spacious parking area and occasionally a bus that appeared to be carrying a group. My impression is of a lively, well-visited place.

There is pottery to look at, and the Mugenan workshop on the same grounds offers pottery experiences. Check the [official workshop information](https://mugenan.co.jp/ceramics/) for activities and booking details.

Above all, I like the coffee. I am not an especially devoted coffee enthusiast, but a cup here still makes me think, “That is good.” Of the places in this guide, it is my personal favorite for coffee.

I visit less often when I need to work. For me, this is somewhere to enjoy a drink and the setting, rather than settle in for a long work session. The café also asks visitors to avoid extended stays when it is busy.

Address: 2697 Inbe. The café and workshop keep different schedules; check the [latest café announcements](https://mugenan.co.jp/cafe-mugenan-plus/) before leaving.

## A Day of Travel, a Day of Everyday Life

Some days call for a quick drink beside the station. Others invite a meal surrounded by pottery, or a slightly longer walk for architecture and greenery.

For me, the pleasure is less in deciding which café is “best” than in finding the one that suits the day. I hope this guide helps you choose your own stop—and enjoy exploring Inbe.
